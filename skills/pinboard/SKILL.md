# Coordinate with the pinboard

Coordinate shared work through one project-local ledger while keeping execution isolated by attempt. Any task may apply an atomic project transition. Preparation and attempt leases fence the longer operations that genuinely need retained authority.

Use [the coding-agent runtime adapters](references/runtime-adapters.md) for identity, checkout isolation, subagents and reviewers, permission declarations, waiting, and optional messaging. This skill owns the shared decisions in both integrations.

## Keep the human's picture current

The human decides what reaches the product and often returns after hours with only your replies to go on. Keep four facts true in them: the **agreed outcome and where its results are meant to land**; **what is happening now** and who acts next, including which task to follow when another task was created or responsibility would otherwise be unclear; **where results live and whether they reach users**, meaning the integration target such as `main`; and **what remains open** for the human. This section owns what the human is told; intake, delivery, parallel-work, and runtime guidance report through it. Spend the human's attention on decisions and material state, not volume.

**Start and change.** Before any implementation or exploration begins, including a throwaway experiment, say in one sentence what will be produced and where it will land, for example “This stays on branch `x`; nothing reaches `main` unless you choose.” For broad work—repository-wide, or spanning several principal product, documentation, agent, tooling, packaging, or CI surfaces—the sentence also names the main surfaces to read and touch, the approximate magnitude, and any surprising exclusion. Give it before a preparation claim, implementation reads, a brief, or a worker launch; in a one-shot client, put it before the next tool call and keep working. It confirms the interpretation and asks no permission. Ask one concrete question only when it exposes an unresolved choice that would materially change product behavior, architecture, scope, compatibility, or verification; the later brief preserves the agreed magnitude. While planning, also apply the [opportunity checkpoint](#resolve-material-architecture-limitations-before-preparation). When one of the four facts later changes—an item closes while its changes stay off `main`, a branch is merged, scope widens, a worker finishes—say so in one line in your next reply.

**Read before you report.** Board states do not say who acts next or where changes live. Before saying what waits on the human, what a review found, whether a worker is running, or whether changes reached users, read:

- for each attempt in review, `pinboard_attempt_inspect` with `reconciliation: null`: `reconcile-repository` means it was reviewed favorably and waits for the human's repository decision, and `review-subagent` means it waits for a reviewer;
- for each active attempt, `pinboard_attempt_authority` operation `status`—an active holder is a running worker, none means nobody is working on it—and its item status `review_verdict`, where `returned-for-correction` means a correction is owed; when its `rebound_since_return` is true the return is history, and [replacement-brief recovery](#recover-after-a-brief-replacement) records a new return before correction;
- for each paused item, its `pinboard_item_status` operation `item` result, whose `pause_reason` says what it waits on;
- in Git, `git branch --no-merged <target>`. For an unmerged branch with no live item, find the items and attempts that own it with `pinboard_item_status` operation `branch`, then read the owning item's status; a terminal item's `closure` says when and by which action it closed, its recorded `outcome_evidence` says whether the human kept the change unmerged and where a kept experiment's scratch board is, and that useful change stays open. For a live item whose branch is already merged, check whether a review covered it and call `pinboard_item_status` with `{"request": {"project_root": "<exact checkout>", "work_root": "<exact work root>", "operation": "integration", "item_id": "<owning item>", "target": "<named target>"}}` for `pinboard-item-integration/v1` content evidence, including rebase and squash merges. A remote-tracking target is read locally without fetching; remote freshness remains separately observed.

Account for every unmerged branch as open, owned by current work, or deliberately set aside. Leave an unread fact out rather than narrating what you did not check, and never infer it from a state label. These reads are yours: the reply names their conclusions, never their files, commands, or tools.

**Check what the message presupposes.** When the human's message assumes a state—that work is in `main`, was reviewed, is saved, or that a work item is underway—verify it with the cheapest current read before building on it, and correct it first when it is wrong. Read a request to include, keep, or preserve useful work as a request to bring it into the product: say first whether it is in the product, then where it lives, what would bring it there—usually integrating the already reviewed change rather than redoing it—and the one choice needed. “Already covered” or “already recorded” alone never answers it.

**Status words name their subject.** Pinboard lifecycle words describe the board, not the product. Say what `done`, `complete`, `closed`, `saved`, `covered`, `reviewed`, or `merged` covers, and for changes whether they reach users: “The item is closed on the board; its change is only on branch `x`, not in `main`.” Reserve unqualified `complete`, `completed`, `finished`, and `done` for a terminal work item. Every review claim names the exact candidate, commits, or PR head it covered; after a publication or merge, say whether that review covered exactly the published or merged commits, and state remote identity as observed or unverified. A change that reached the integration target without a covering review stays open: say it is in `main` but unreviewed. When items or evidence live on a scratch or disposable board, say so.

**Still open.** At a stopping point, end with one short `Still open:` line only when something the human could forget remains open: a decision they owe, useful changes not in the integration target, a change in it without a covering review, review not yet done, an unresolved repository disposition, a paused item waiting for them, or a worker still running. When the human asks what else is open or is about to leave, include every such item from the reads above, not only the current topic's. When they ask about one item, answer about it and mention other items only there, one short clause each, each read as above. Omit the line when nothing is open, or when the reply already asks or lists everything that is open. Never list routine agent-owned steps in it. Keep an entry in later replies, even after a topic change or after its item becomes terminal, until the human resolves it or explicitly sets it aside. This lives in the conversation; do not create a reminder store or scheduler.

**Ask only where being wrong is costly.** Request explicit acknowledgement only when a wrong assumption would be costly or hard to undo: where work lands, an irreversible or externally visible effect such as a merge, push, publication, or deletion, or a change to agreed scope. Phrase it as your stated default so a short reply suffices: “I'll keep it on its branch unless you want it in `main`.” Decide routine and reversible choices yourself and continue. Permission to experiment is neither a commitment to merge nor a decision to discard useful results; an experiment may end in isolation when that is the agreed outcome.

**Lead with decisions; keep mechanics invisible.** Put the decision or material state first and state each fact once; never repeat “not in `main`” or a pending decision elsewhere in the same reply. Raise at most two decisions per reply, each with your recommendation, and give every other open item your default in a few words. Keep a status answer under about 150 words and offer detail instead of including it; add method detail only when it changes the result, confidence, risk, or next step. Keep Pinboard mechanics out of replies unless the human asks to inspect or troubleshoot Pinboard: storage and evidence files such as `review.md`, revisions, leases, claims, preparation or worker authority and its release or revocation, generations, receipts, snapshots, hash or diff comparisons, the Git and tool commands you ran, action and schema names, briefs being published, reviewed, or rebound, generated views, validation, unchanged state, stale-action refreshes, rejected reads, and routine retries. Use the human's words—goal, agreed scope, branch, review—rather than Pinboard vocabulary such as definition, obligation, checkout policy, attempt, or reconciliation, and translate mechanics into who acts next. Report a recovery only when it fails or repeats, changes the command or resource, broadens permission beyond one narrowly approved retry, changes state unexpectedly, costs material time, or needs the human. Invisibility never hides the four facts above.

## Resolve material architecture limitations before preparation

Before acquiring a preparation claim for any relevant refinement or change, inspect the project's current limitations owner and apply its reusable limitation method. Distinguish an acknowledged existing limitation, an introduced or widened limitation, a defect against accepted behavior, and a speculative risk. Trace a proposed local remedy to its foundation so a compensating layer does not preserve, mask, or deepen an unacknowledged material limitation.

When the proposed work crosses a previously acknowledged limitation without materially changing its consequence, preserve that accepted boundary without asking again. When it would introduce, widen, preserve, mask, or deepen an unacknowledged material limitation, resolve the decision before claiming preparation authority. Generic task permission and silence are insufficient.

Present the decision in this information order:

**Architecture checkpoint**

1. Explain the concrete behavior, affected supported paths, consequences, and observable reopening condition.
2. Separate the exact unlock from work that remains outside scope.
3. Give the recommendation and its evidence.

**Practical consequence: state plainly what accepting this exact limitation means for the product and future work.**

Ask an ordinary approval question about that exact decision. An explicit natural-language answer is sufficient; never require a recited acknowledgement phrase. Approval authorizes only the stated limitation, not related implementation or hardening beyond accepted scope. Record the resolved assessment in the complete accepted definition and existing brief fields. Do not acquire or retain a preparation lease while waiting for this decision.

**Opportunity checkpoint**

The architecture checkpoint keeps a limitation from being accepted silently; its counterpart keeps an agreement from silently blocking a better result. Whenever you ideate, plan, or prepare, before implementation starts, test each agreement in play—a scope limit, constraint, dependency, pass bar, or design choice, including one the human just stated—against the best outcome you can see, such as one the recorded item already describes. When an agreement blocks a clearly better outcome, say so once: name it, say whether the human or you introduced it, and give the gain and cost of revising it, as a suggestion the human may accept or decline. Constraints you introduced deserve this most, since the human may hold them loosely. For example: “You asked for X. Y would also give Z, at the cost of W; I'll plan X unless you prefer Y.” Keep working under the agreement until the human decides; the suggestion changes no scope by itself. Accepted scope stays fixed during implementation: a better idea found then follows the assurance or intake route below, never the current attempt.

## Keep assurance inside accepted product scope

Before making an assurance obligation mandatory in preparation, implementation, or review, classify it as an in-scope defect, an accepted assurance obligation, a material new assurance obligation, or a speculative risk. A mandatory obligation or blocking finding must trace to accepted scope or an exact accepted brief contract or criterion, an applicable repository policy, an exact reviewed product-authority family, a named existing production consumer, or an observed failure on a supported path. A broad appeal to correctness, an exploratory failing test, public product prose, or guidance written by the current change cannot authorize a new guarantee by itself.

Fix defects and enforce accepted assurance without asking the human to confirm scope again. Keep speculative risks non-blocking. When a finding would materially add a concurrency, durability, security, adversarial, platform, or compatibility guarantee without one of the accepted bases above, stop only the affected preparation, implementation, or review disposition. The task that owns the outcome presents one concise decision containing the current product behavior, proposed new guarantee, triggering scenario, evidence gap, likely implementation and verification footprint, recommendation, and practical consequence. Ask one ordinary question about that exact expansion; silence and generic task authority are insufficient.

Use the current definition and canonical brief fields to preserve the decision and its provenance. If those fields cannot express the distinction, stop before changing the schema and state the exact schema, codec, projection, tool-contract, review-job, compatibility, and architecture footprint that would be required. Newly accepted assurance then follows the ordinary definition, brief, candidate, and review replacement path; an unaccepted expansion does not become implementation work or a blocking review defect.

## Match detail to the question

User-facing progress and receipts describe the user's tasks, decisions, confidence, blockers, and next actions under [the human's picture](#keep-the-humans-picture-current). The implementation route is the one advance choice to disclose after a human change request.

Apply an engaged, neutral collaboration style whenever Pinboard is active, regardless of which repository or product the user is discussing. Advance product reasoning in decision-sized increments. Make the smallest useful move, preserve the difference between a proposal, an accepted decision, an assumption, and an unresolved question, then stop only where the user's answer can materially change scope, meaning, emphasis, or structure. Resolve obvious wording, organization, and routine workflow choices without asking. Do not jump from an early idea to a complete polished plan that nobody has accepted.

When a Pinboard-managed request clearly creates or substantially revises a technical document, report, RFC, design note, investigation or decision record, pull-request description, or comparable human-facing artifact, also use `$technical-writing`; an explicit invocation selects it for ambiguous document work. Do not load or announce it for routine status, intake, lifecycle, implementation, or review coordination. It owns document composition; this skill retains accepted decisions and the surrounding interaction.

Readable artifacts are outcome context, not mechanics. Whenever a reply or permitted task message mentions a Pinboard item or another user-relevant fact with confirmed readable Markdown—accepted-definition summaries, briefs and attempt views, implementation results, blockers, complete reviews, history evidence, commits, pull requests, and comparable durable results—make its human-facing name or purpose a native clickable link on that mention, every time. Use the actual item, attempt, history, or evidence selector exposed by the selected work root and verify that it exists; never synthesize a target from an assumed directory or filename convention. Without readable Markdown, mention the fact accurately without a guessed link. Announce a newly available or materially refreshed artifact promptly with its purpose; later mentions link it without repeating its contents or adding unchanged-status notices. Canonical JSON and immutable storage copies are mechanics, not readable artifacts.

Do not suppress a higher-level instruction to disclose the first use of a selected skill; keep it to one concise, outcome-oriented acknowledgment per skill, without a separate Pinboard explanation of companion-skill selection, internal routing, or healthy task state.

A disjoint ready or active item is not a user-facing event; start the current task normally and mention another task only when observed write overlap, live contention, changed priority, or required user action affects the current outcome.

Default “where do we stand?”, “quick status”, and equivalent orientation questions to the single `overview` result plus the reads that [the human's picture](#keep-the-humans-picture-current) requires. Lead with what waits on the human, then work in progress with who acts next, then ready items in saved order with dependency reasons. Give each item one line of at most twelve words—the item, where it stands in the human's terms, and your default when it waits on them—and leave out facts you did not read. Those lines are the answer's open items, so a status answer has no `Still open:` line and asks at most one question. Link every mentioned item under the rule above. Intake candidates already appear in that order. The revision stamp is freshness evidence, not reply content. Do not read history, project notes, GitHub, CI, delivery state, or old decisions for this default answer, or acquire a lease merely to explain the live picture.

On a task's first mention in a conversation, add one short practical clause from its overview title, effect, and unlock unless the immediate context already supplies it. When the overview includes a planned-replacement warning, state the exact replacement item and practical replacement cost; do not infer replacement from similar titles or prose. Ask the human whether to revise or withdraw the relation, stop the affected work, or retain it temporarily; never select the outcome on their behalf. Temporary retention records only the rationale the human supplies and the exact displayed replacement cost.

Use `complete:<attempt>` only when the entire work-item outcome is accepted, the current v4 checkpoint disposition is `terminal`, the exact candidate is protected and independently reviewed, and every already-authorized repository disposition and cleanup effect has succeeded; keep a favorable final candidate in review while those effects run. When an independently buildable checkpoint is accepted and the canonical brief disposition is `continue`, use `accept-checkpoint:<attempt>` with the stable checkpoint ID, exact candidate identity, and nonempty review evidence; it archives the exact current `result.md` and `review.md`, pauses the same item and attempt, and fences worker authority. Publish the revised canonical JSON brief at its next artifact revision, then use the ordinary `resume:<item>` with that accepted reference before dispatch. When the protected candidate is accepted but work should continue within the same active attempt, use `accept-review-and-continue:<attempt>` with that exact candidate identity and nonempty acceptance evidence; it returns the item and attempt to active, clears the protected candidate, records the accepted candidate and evidence without a checkpoint, sets the next action to continue, and fences the previous worker authority. When review finds an actionable defect, use `return-for-correction:<attempt>` with one concise reason that identifies the durable review evidence, and capture the successful transition's exact `history_id`. Select project dispatch again and commission a fresh independent source review—bound to the accepted brief for a local checkpoint, or to the effective checkpoint and its current contract review for a cross-boundary one—and pass it with the selected return to the matching correction dispatch leaf before launching the fresh correction worker. The same attempt and evidence remain; the new worker reacquires at a higher generation through the rooted MCP calls in its generated prompt.

Treat the paused state produced by an accepted intermediate checkpoint as an internal evidence boundary when the human has already authorized continued execution, the brief records remaining work, and no blocker or unresolved human-owned decision exists. Report the accepted milestone, then continue the owning turn through any already-selected repository disposition, next brief publication and binding, resume, fresh worker acquisition, and delivery. Preserve the pause, immutable evidence archive, and authority fence; stop only for an explicit request to pause, a genuine blocker, missing authority, or an unresolved human-owned decision.

## Interpret the available actions

Translate executable actions into ordinary repository and product language; the exact action record serves execution only. Whenever the explanation names an item, decision, or evidence artifact with confirmed readable Markdown, apply the shared readable-artifact link rule.

When selecting work, explain:

1. the current product or repository trajectory;
2. what changed after the last accepted item;
3. which items are ready and why;
4. which items are blocked and the exact unresolved condition;
5. which ordering is mechanically determined by dependencies;
6. which choice genuinely requires human priority;
7. the recommended next item and why now.

Respect the saved live priority order from `overview` before making a heuristic recommendation. Its `next_unstarted` context names the first unstarted item and live dependencies; read the matching item row for lifecycle, replacement and preparation facts, and report an existing blocker rather than silently recommending a lower item. Active, review and previously started attempts remain current work, not new starts. Priority does not change dependencies, preparation authority, available actions or independent parallel eligibility.

Save only an explicit human priority decision through `pinboard_order`: put the complete current live sequence from `pinboard_overview` in `order.expected_order` and the complete authorized permutation in `order.requested_order`, with `order.schema: pinboard-live-order/v1`, exact roots, and trusted `actor_task_id`/`actor_host_id` inside `request`, preserving unmentioned relative order unless the human decided otherwise. A stale rejection needs a fresh overview and reconciliation with the same decision; a committed warning keeps the receipt and names exact CLI view-repair recovery, never order replay. A fresh overview verifies saved order, but after a lost reply it cannot prove this caller committed, because a valid no-op leaves the same order; never blindly replay the mutation.

When the human asks for a new priority recommendation, prefer, in order:

1. evidence preservation or avoidance of an irreversible transition;
2. prerequisites required by the current product objective;
3. runtime, consumer, or user-visible product evidence;
4. foundation work that is concretely cheaper now;
5. optional hardening only after its recorded reopen condition occurs.

Do not turn that ordering into a score. A recommendation remains an explanation, not an automatic product decision.

## Apply a transition

Before the first default initialization or when a routine mutation reports `SQLITE_READONLY`, follow the shared runtime adapter's [Codex protected project writes](references/runtime-adapters.md#codex-protected-project-writes) rule. Keep one-time Git exclusion authority separate from routine project-data access.

1. Identify one action as `<kind>:<subject>`. Read its returned `use_case`, `effect`, `permitted_roles`, `subject_kind`, `lifecycle_precondition`, and `practical_result` instead of inferring from the verb. The stable `effect` field says whether the action accepts a lifecycle transition payload; it does not summarize every outer command effect, and dispatch may publish or reuse review evidence while leaving lifecycle unchanged. Treat the exact action record's `authorization` as the runtime authority to forward; the kind-level permitted roles do not replace it. When semantic input is not already settled, use the advertised exact transition leaf schema and prepare its structured payload before selecting the action. `revise-item:<item>` replaces one complete definition after exact revision-and-digest matching; never synthesize a partial patch. `report-blocker:<attempt>` is a worker advisory with no mutation payload; `block:<attempt>` is the project transition for an active attempt; `block-item:<item>` is the project transition for a ready item without an attempt. Blocking may name only dependencies already accepted in the current definition and never changes dependencies itself.
2. For one project change, call `pinboard_actions` with exact roots, role `project`, and structured `action_id` containing the exact kind and subject. Copy its returned action identity and `subject_revision` together as the transition receipt; supply the invoking task and host identity.
3. For an attempt- or preparation-local change, call `pinboard_actions` with exact roots, role `worker` or `preparer`, exact lease and generation, and structured action identity. Copy the returned identity and revision together. Forward the lease and generation unchanged; do not infer authorization from a neighboring role or reconstruct a partial receipt from prose.
4. Call `pinboard_transition` with those exact roots, role, receipt, authority fields, and structured leaf payload. A committed result returns the exact action identity, committed revision, history receipt, effect, changed surfaces, retry disposition, and any generated-view warning. A warning does not undo the authoritative transition. Preserve that receipt and call `pinboard_attempt_inspect` for the derived continuation; never replay a committed transition to obtain it.
5. On `STALE_ACTION`, discard the receipt, reselect only the same exact action, and retry silently while its semantics and payload are unchanged, retained state is safe, and existing authorization can refresh or reacquire the capability. Stop when the action disappears, its semantics, role, or payload changes, authority cannot be refreshed, state is invalid, or the lease or turn deadline cannot fit another attempt, and report only the resulting blocker, changed outcome, unsafe state, or required decision. A freshly selected receipt rejected without an intervening ledger revision is an executable contradiction: preserve the evidence and report it.
6. On `ACTION_NOT_AVAILABLE`, do not replay the same command, switch roles, or skip to another lifecycle state. Validate once and refresh only the same exact action under fresh authority. If it disappeared, follow the resulting state and report it only when the user's outcome or next action changed. If it is now available, apply its fresh receipt once under the `STALE_ACTION` rule; treat only an unexplained rejection without an intervening ledger revision as an executable contradiction.
7. If a task is interrupted before a receipt is visible, never replay its retained action. Follow the interrupted-transition recovery in `references/state-and-recovery.md`; authoritative state distinguishes no change from one complete committed transition, and a fresh lease generation fences the abandoned action.
8. Report the practical effect of the returned transition in terms of the human's picture. Run `<launcher-root>/scripts/pinboard validate` immediately after any non-tool edit to supporting attempt artifacts.

The executable rejects stale definition identities, expired or replaced lease holders, illegal states, invalid dependencies, and inconsistent references. Actions that require current accepted scope remain unavailable to attempts pinned to an older definition; rebind is the explicit active-or-paused recovery that accepts a matching current-definition brief and corrected Git lineage together. It does not decide whether evidence is true, whether work is valuable, or what product behavior should mean.

For an explicit terminal human decision about live work without an accepted attempt, discover the exact project `close` action through `pinboard_actions`, then call `pinboard_transition` with its receipt, structured outcome and reason, current actor identities, and exact roots instead of manufacturing lifecycle states. The tool applies one direct atomic project transition. Do not add an overview preflight. Use `done` when the recorded decision or outcome is complete and may satisfy dependents. Use `dropped` only when the work is intentionally abandoned and has no live dependents. Never use `close` to bypass review for an accepted attempt, including after pause or block. Resume or rebind the same attempt and keep its normal evidence-backed review path; a genuine change to accepted semantics requires deliberate item revision, matching brief review, and rebind. The returned revision is the durable mutation receipt, so do not create a temporary payload file or replay the transition to prove that it committed. Still perform the fresh exact `pinboard_item_status` operation `item` read required by the workflow-ending reply guidance below.

## Safe boundaries

Process newly delivered intake only after the current review, transition, commit, or other atomic repository action ends. Delivery does not authorize interrupting or widening an active attempt.

Proposal creation persists immutable facts and a same-identity ready item. It appends at the back by default or uses an explicitly requested one-based position, and relation semantics may add a dependency or proposal origin. It creates no attempt, pauses no current work, and completes nothing. When intake is embedded in ongoing work, keep a compact continuation anchor in the current task context before invoking it:

- the pre-intake objective;
- the next action already promised;
- the exact active or paused item, attempt, proposal, or durable selector that owns the facts after context compaction.

After persistence and optional notification handling, return to that anchor in the same turn. Re-read the durable selector when compaction made conversational memory uncertain; do not create a scheduler or recovery record. Before the final response, reconcile every announced pending action as completed, durably deferred at an exact owner, or blocked by one exact decision. The anchor is the agent's own state; what the human sees is the receipt below and, when something remains open for them, the `Still open:` line.

Conditional follow-up language such as “if this is a production defect, follow it up” authorizes one bounded disposition only after current evidence proves the named condition. If it is false or unproved, make no ledger write; if the exact observation and consequence are already recorded, reuse that owner. If the condition is proved and the exact concern is new, create at most one `follow-up` or `independent` intake proposal through `$pinboard-intake`, choosing the relation from the evidence, and never turn this authority into a prerequisite, preparation, activation, implementation, notification, or unrelated intake. Record the evidence, relation to current work, and smallest useful next decision, then return to the continuation anchor.

Deliberate steering is distinct from intake. Insert a genuine prerequisite only at a safe boundary, pause or block the displaced objective through its legal transition, and name the ordinary `resume:<item>` action that restores it. Recovery is for interrupted or inconsistent state, not the normal route back to preserved work.

### Reconcile material concerns before reporting them

Before presenting a discovered out-of-scope concern as planned, preserved, covered, queued, deferred, or future work, determine the disposition of that exact concern. A broader item with a compatible theme is not exact coverage.

Use one of these durable dispositions:

- `already recorded`: the exact observation and its consequence were durable before the current report;
- `recorded now`: the exact observation was absent and this turn added it to its authoritative item context or created an intake proposal;
- `not recorded`: no exact durable owner exists, persistence was not authorized, or persistence failed.

When the concern belongs to an existing admitted item but its accepted semantics are incomplete, read the current definition and discover the project `revise-item` action and call `pinboard_transition` with one complete replacement, exact expected identity, source task, reason, receipt, and roots. A retained attempt then remains pinned and stale; use its legal review-correction path when a candidate is protected, publish a matching revised canonical brief, and rebind the active or paused attempt before continuation. When no admitted item owns the concern, use `$pinboard-intake` if the user has explicitly authorized preservation.

Keep current work as the main topic and lead the receipt with its practical outcome, not the internal disposition name:

- For `already recorded`, say `Saved for later — <concern> is already in <exact owner, state, and board>; current work <continues | is blocked by it>.` For `recorded now`, say `Saved for later — <concern> is now in <exact owner, state, and board>; current work <continues | is blocked by it>.` Saved means recorded, not started or scheduled.
- For an explicit dismissal, say `Not saved — <concern> was dismissed at your request; no follow-up remains.`
- For completed work, say `Completed — <result and where it lives>; no follow-up needed.` Do not introduce completed or dismissed work as a durable concern or saved follow-up.

A concern about useful work that has not reached the product is an adoption question, not a recording question; answer it under [the presupposition check](#keep-the-humans-picture-current).

When an exact owner or completed result in these forms has confirmed readable Markdown, make its human-facing label the link under the shared rule.

A material `not recorded` disposition is unresolved and must not end as a bare receipt. State the concern, exact cause, durable state, current-work impact, and next action owner. Then produce exactly one resolution:

- If preservation lacks authorization, ask one concrete preserve-or-dismiss question: `This needs a decision — <concern> is not saved because <cause>. Current work <impact>. Should I preserve it for later or dismiss it?`
- If a safe retry needs no new authority and does not change the outcome, retry silently before the terminal response. If that retry cannot finish, report the persistence failure and its current-work impact once.
- If retry needs new authority, changes scope, or overrides another owner, ask one concrete approval question that names the action and consequence.
- If persistence still fails after the permitted retry, report the same four facts and ask the one decision that can actually resolve it. Never leave `not recorded` as a terminal aside.

Let `recorded now` mean this turn before the update. For `already recorded`, include the earlier durable selector or timestamp. Use separate labeled fields only when the compact outcome-first line cannot make a persistence failure, ambiguous owner, blocking impact, or required decision unmistakable.

Never use a later edit to imply earlier coverage. If a broad item existed before the report but the exact observation was added only after a question or challenge, say both facts explicitly.

When an active attempt discovers a prerequisite:

1. preserve its current result and verification;
2. use `$pinboard-intake` to propose the prerequisite;
3. report the advisory `report-blocker:<attempt>` from the worker context, then have the owning task select the exact project transition: use `block:<attempt>` only when its named dependencies already exist in the current definition, or `pause:<attempt>` when preserving work without such a condition; revise the complete definition first when a newly accepted dependency is required, and never use the intake-only `block-item:<item>` action for active work;
4. implement the accepted prerequisite independently from the current integration base;
5. resume the preserved attempt only after its freshness check passes.

Never absorb the prerequisite silently into the current attempt.

### Capture and reconcile consequential invocation evidence

For an initialized project, the ignored `.pinboard/contributor-traces.config` decides whether normal Pinboard invocations are captured. Its `unsafe_persist_exact_pinboard_traces` value is created explicitly `off`; an item override of `inherit`, `on`, or `off` changes the effective mode for that item across tasks and worktrees, and the runtime rereads it before each supported CLI or MCP invocation. Effective on declares that exact arguments and results are safe to persist locally without redaction. Automatic traces are private ignored files under `.pinboard/invocation-traces/` with newest-100 retention. Manual capture remains available through the CLI `--capture-evidence <file> --safe-to-persist-exactly --` wrapper or a dedicated MCP `--capture-evidence-dir <existing-directory> --safe-to-persist-exactly` process, with caller-owned destination and retention and no environment snapshot. [Contributor guidance](../../CONTRIBUTING.md#diagnose-pinboard-invocations-during-contributor-work) owns setup, toggling, risk and deletion details.

Treat captured files as private evidence, not accepted ledger truth. CLI records preserve the complete launcher argv, original stdout and stderr bytes, and exit status; MCP records preserve the exact SDK-decoded arguments and strictly validated result, without transport bytes or pre-callback client events. A strict result-validation failure preserves an unavailable record with the callback classification and commit reference before the error is re-raised. Distinguish pre-publication `capture-unavailable` from `capture-committed-with-warning`, whose filename retains the published digest and size after a post-publication failure. Never replay a mutation to replace missing, interrupted or externally truncated evidence.

During any work on an enabled Pinboard project or item, investigate consequential Pinboard anomalies when they arise, regardless of the current work item or Codex task. Routine success stays quiet. Inspect the relevant trace, but report selectors and conclusions rather than raw values that may contain secrets. Reconcile evidence at a natural task or review boundary, consolidate recurrence, and keep one compact record for each distinct consequential observation with:

- attempted outcome;
- observed behavior, exact evidence selector when available, and confidence in the conclusion;
- recovery outcome;
- measured command, time or token cost when available, without inventing absent measurements;
- classification as Pinboard product behavior or discovery, evidence handling, caller or harness error, runtime or environment constraint, external client behavior, or model limitation;
- disposition and rationale;
- observable reopening condition for deferred or accepted friction.

Use one explicit disposition: fixed and verified in authorized scope; incorporated into an exact admitted item whose accepted definition covers it; admitted separately with saved priority; deferred because cost, uncertainty or an external constraint prevents a useful remedy; accepted friction with proportionate demonstrated recovery; or unresolved because evidence is insufficient. An actionable Pinboard-owned or project-owned observation is not disposed until it is fixed and verified or an exact admitted item owns it with a saved priority decision. When that ownership needs human authority, ask one admission or priority question at the existing result or review boundary instead of leaving a TODO or creating another workflow stage.

Failures use `pinboard-mcp-item-status-result/v3`.

Distinguish a PR published for the human to review from a candidate presented as ready to merge. Unless the human requested the review handoff, resolve the required review by a separate coding agent before inviting a merge decision. Report hosted checks as observed or unverified; neither PR publication nor local code equivalence supplies them.

Before commissioning a caller-selected checkpoint review, require accepted candidate bytes as well as the package reference. Never reconstruct a different patch, attach bytes without selected history, strengthen historical assurance, permit a patch-only new correction start, or replay a committed recovery.

Checkpoint acceptance for a new candidate verifies its protected identity before publishing candidate, result, implementation review, and the v3 package as one evidence closure. A working-tree candidate binds the actual full HEAD revision and exact `git diff --binary HEAD` bytes; a commit candidate binds the full current HEAD with a clean working tree and the exact `git diff --binary <accepted-brief-base> <current-HEAD>` bytes. Validation, review, project export, and covered completion stop when the current package's candidate reference, identity, or canonical bytes are missing or mismatched. Retained v1/v2 evidence stays historical without relabeling or strengthening its assurance; a retained v1 working-tree snapshot with a known exact preimage remains restorable, but its patch-only identity cannot authorize a new correction start.

Treat worker completion as a review request, not acceptance. Compare the attempt brief, diff or commit, result receipt, and fresh proportionate verification. Start with the changed and explicitly uncertain semantic surface: reuse unchanged reviewed relationships, revalidate changed relationships and documentation, and classify every changed file or contract preparation did not cover. Widen only for architecture, persistence, wire, lifecycle, dynamic-consumer, or demonstrated-blind-spot evidence, not merely because the checkpoint is cross-boundary. Check the architecture declaration against the final owner and dependency-direction diff: `none` must be truthful, `read-only` must conform to its named authority, and `update-required` must include a coherent same-candidate authority change. Account for every acceptance criterion, Contract row and authorization basis, coverage row, and lifecycle sibling row. When the checkpoint invokes the [developer-navigation lens](../repository-readiness/references/developer-navigation.md), verify the final representative trace, sibling edit sites, retained exhaustive owners, and dynamic-wiring failure mode against the frozen candidate. Apply the assurance classification above: every blocking finding cites accepted scope or an exact brief contract or criterion, an applicable repository rule, an exact reviewed product-authority family, a named production consumer, or an observed supported-path failure, never broad correctness prose, an exploratory failure, public narrative, or newly written guidance. Fix defects and enforce accepted hardening without redundant confirmation, and route a materially broader unsupported guarantee to the one human decision above. Classify each finding as an implementation defect, brief omission, authority contradiction, product decision, or new capability. A correction review may reuse package evidence only when its owner and relevant relationships remain valid; it revalidates changed owners, marks relationship-invalidated neighbors stale even with unchanged hashes, and resolves every prior finding before one complete verdict. Complete the item only when every acceptance criterion is satisfied and current knowledge owners are reconciled. A `continue` checkpoint may be accepted or returned to active reviewed work, never terminally completed. A `terminal` checkpoint may complete only from protected review with the exact candidate, current result and review digests, independent reviewer identity, and one ascending compact reused-or-revalidated row for every enumerated checkpoint, including an empty row set when none was previously accepted. Focused completion supplies the strict v2 input; broad action discovery intentionally omits completion. Retained v3/v2 briefs must first be replaced by an independently reviewed v4 brief and rebound or resumed. Later mentions follow the readable-artifact rule; do not link immutable copies archived by acceptance transitions.

While a candidate is in review—even after a favorable verdict—return it for correction with a reason saying accepted scope superseded it rather than that the implementation is defective, then revise, publish, rebind, implement, submit, and review again. Never describe an ownerless concern as saved.

Carry out obvious authorized remaining work before asking the human to act. Ask one concrete question only when the next step needs a product, architecture, requirements, limitation, or material-risk decision, or genuinely missing authority, including for repository disposition, cleanup, terminal transition, or bounded blocker recovery; otherwise name the agent-owned next step and continue it. Once fresh status reports a terminal work item and all authorized repository effects succeeded, state that no further action remains and whether its changes reached the integration target. Omit closure framing from ordinary discussion and healthy in-progress updates.
