# Work-item consolidation

Load this phase only when the human requests combining saved work. The [main conversation core](../SKILL.md) owns decisions and replies. The preview below is read-only until separately approved.

## Preview work-item consolidation

When a user wants to combine saved work, first ask them to identify the live items to compare if the selection is not already explicit. An optional proposed owner is part of that selection. Keep this first pass read-only: it recommends a smaller backlog but changes no item, relation, priority, proposal, attempt, or accepted artifact. Do not use `merge-proposal` to combine accepted work-item definitions; it only disposes an intake proposal.

1. Read the current overview for live order, direct dependencies, planned replacements, preparation, and attempts, plus the complete current definition and exact status of each selected item and optional owner. Record each item's ID, definition revision and digest, lifecycle state, and sampled project revision in the map; a missing or terminal selection cannot support a current preview. These reads are not one atomic snapshot: reconcile their project revisions, refresh changed selections and affected live relationships, or label the comparison stale and stop short of an assured recommendation. Do not read unrelated retained history or use generated item views as definition authority.
2. Compare the selected objectives, effects, obligations, scope, criteria, exclusions, dependencies, evidence, and stopping or reopening conditions. For each carried obligation, propose its exact recorded `origin`: source `item_id`, `definition_revision` and `obligation_id`. Give every obligation and distinct material scope fact exactly one proposed disposition: owner outcome, named checkpoint, retained independent item, already covered item, or explicit dismissal. Preserve material scope facts with their source revision and location even when they are not definition obligations. Use `pinboard_obligation_coverage` for existing carrying claims instead of inferring them from titles, text equality or export comparisons; an uncovered row remains visible. Keep conflicting claims, uncertain evidence, and unmatched facts visible, and retain a concern independently while its disposition is uncertain.
3. Recommend one owner for one concrete product outcome, or one [campaign](#shape-a-campaign) owner for several outcomes that share a scarce resource or execution window, or that the human prefers to run as fewer, longer stretches. A checkpoint must deliver independent value and leave nonempty work toward the owner's outcome. Keep separate ownership when a consumer needs a result before the owner could deliver it, or when different stopping conditions, uncertainty, or reopening triggers would make one outcome wait on work the human did not choose to bundle. If no coherent owner exists, say so and show the completed map rather than forcing a merge.
4. Show what applying the map would do to every affected dependency edge and live queue position, including downstream items found through the overview, with each planned-replacement cost and any preparation claim or active, paused, or review attempt that constrains revision or closure; proposed absorption cannot bypass current work, its pinned definition, or independent review. Name the exact action IDs currently available for each affected subject and a conditional order for later application; `revise-item`, `record-replacement`, `rebind-attempt`, `close`, and the review or completion actions each retain their own legality. For an unavailable action, explain the prerequisite instead of inventing a consolidation mutation.
5. Present the concern map and impacts as a proposal and ask for a separate decision before any application. Show the human one row per absorbed item, with its checkpoint, what it waits on and any exception, and list individual obligations only where their disposition differs from being carried by the owner; keep the complete per-obligation map for application. After approval, reread exact current definitions, statuses, overview relationships, and fresh action receipts; changed identities require a revised map. Apply only the approved changes through their existing actions in the stated order, recording carried obligations' exact origins in proposal v3 or definition v3. Keep origin-free obligations explicit with `origin: null` in those forms; origin-free definitions use v2. After writes, read named-pair coverage and the current planned-replacement report, and reconcile every uncovered obligation with the approved map. Before ending absorbed work, follow [replacement coverage](setup-and-transitions.md#read-replacement-coverage), including an explicit human disposition for uncovered work. A completed replacement remains a supported coverage source; its recorded carrying claims do not replace independent completion or close authority.

A retrospective portfolio comparison can illustrate a recommendation, but label its historical definitions and lifecycle facts as retrospective. Dogfood the procedure separately on an explicit current live cohort and record whether the ledger revision stayed unchanged during that preview; concurrent work may advance the revision, so attribute only observed effects rather than treating equal revisions as the sole proof.

## Shape a campaign

A campaign is one consolidated item that one owner runs autonomously for a long stretch, so the human starts it once and is needed only at grouped sittings and decision batches. After the human approves the map in step 5, build it as a new item through `pinboard_proposal_create` with a `pinboard-proposal/v3` proposal:

- Carry every absorbed obligation with its exact `origin`. Prefix obligation IDs per source item so they stay unique, and keep equal statements from different sources as separate obligations, because each origin covers only its own source.
- Add the way-of-working obligations with `origin: null`, naming the project's scarce resource and its human steps concretely:
  1. the first reply after starting or resuming presents the agenda: checkpoint order, offline and live work, every planned human sitting and what each decision unblocks;
  2. the brief declares the named checkpoints in order, each reviewed before work that consumes it relies on it;
  3. one owner holds the scarce resource and performs only resource-bound steps, while helpers do the rest;
  4. human hands-on steps are batched into as few sittings as possible;
  5. work continues on everything not waiting; when only waiting work remains, the owner pauses with every pending step and decision in one record instead of waiting;
  6. decisions are presented as one batch per pause, each with a recommendation;
  7. reports state results against the accepted outcome, and a case the brief excluded is not reported as unchecked work unless it changes a decision the human must make.
- Record in `evidence` each absorbed item with its definition revision, so its scope, non-scope and acceptance criteria stay binding context, and copy its material exclusions. Record the human's standing instructions there in their own words, such as landing every checkpoint on one branch for one publication, the publication route, cleanup and closing absorbed items; they authorize the outer workflow and are not obligations.

Then record each absorbed item's planned replacement to the campaign and read its coverage. Absorbed items stay live as placeholders until the campaign completes and close then under the human's decision; ordinary close records `done`, so never close one while its work is outstanding. Leave later items' dependencies on the placeholders; retarget one to a campaign checkpoint only when the brief declares that checkpoint and the consumer could actually start earlier, for example because it does not need the campaign's scarce resource. The human then starts the campaign with an ordinary start request.
