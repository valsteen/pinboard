# Work-item consolidation

Use this section only when the human requests combining saved work. The [main conversation core](../startup/parts/common.md) owns decisions and replies. The preview below is read-only until separately approved.

Prepare the complete technical map for the native operation, then explain the proposal in the human's work names: what comes together, what each checkpoint delivers, what waits, and what they need to decide. Keep obligation and origin inventories, placeholder or coverage terminology, action IDs, hashes and payload keys in preparation and application instructions. Include an exact locator in a human reply only when an existing concrete decision or their explicit request needs it.

## Preview work-item consolidation

When a user wants to combine saved work, first ask them to identify the live items to compare if the selection is not already explicit. An optional proposed owner is part of that selection. Keep this first pass read-only: it recommends a smaller backlog but changes no item, relation, priority, proposal, attempt, or accepted artifact. Do not use `merge-proposal` to combine accepted work-item definitions; it only disposes an intake proposal.

1. Read the complete current definition and exact status of each selected item and optional owner for live position, direct dependencies, planned replacements, preparation, and attempts. Use a complete same-revision [bounded discovery traversal](status-and-selection.md#bounded-discovery) only when the comparison needs the whole live order. Record each item's ID, definition revision and digest, lifecycle state, and sampled project revision in the map; a missing or terminal selection cannot support a current preview. These reads are not one atomic snapshot: reconcile their project revisions, refresh changed selections and affected live relationships, or label the comparison stale and stop short of an assured recommendation. Do not read unrelated retained history or use generated item views as definition authority.
2. Compare the selected objectives, effects, obligations, scope, criteria, exclusions, dependencies, evidence, and stopping or reopening conditions. For each carried obligation, propose its exact recorded `origin`: source `item_id`, `definition_revision` and `obligation_id`. Give every obligation and distinct material scope fact exactly one proposed disposition: owner outcome, named checkpoint, retained independent item, already covered item, or explicit dismissal. Preserve material scope facts with their source revision and location even when they are not definition obligations. Use `pinboard_obligation_coverage` for existing carrying claims instead of inferring them from titles, text equality or export comparisons; an uncovered row remains visible. Keep conflicting claims, uncertain evidence, and unmatched facts visible, and retain a concern independently while its disposition is uncertain.
3. Recommend one owner for one concrete product outcome, or one [campaign](#shape-a-campaign) owner for several outcomes that share a scarce resource or execution window, or that the human prefers to run as fewer, longer stretches. A checkpoint must deliver independent value and leave nonempty work toward the owner's outcome. Keep separate ownership when a consumer needs a result before the owner could deliver it, or when different stopping conditions, uncertainty, or reopening triggers would make one outcome wait on work the human did not choose to bundle. If no coherent owner exists, explain which work needs to stay separate and why, retaining the completed map for application rather than forcing a merge.
4. Build the complete proposed owner definition, then call `pinboard_consolidate` with `request.operation: preview`, exact roots, your current `actor_task_id`, and `selection`. Its `sources` list names each live source and concrete `replacement_cost`; `campaign` names the new or existing `item_id`, `create` boolean and complete current v2/v3 `definition`. Supply the owner's own work and way-of-working obligations there; the operation appends every selected source obligation with a unique ID, unchanged statement and deferral policy, and its direct item/revision/obligation origin. `checkpoint_targets` is empty for whole-campaign waits, or names each outside `consumer`, declared campaign `checkpoint` and `covers_carried_work` explanation for an explicitly proposed earlier wait. The preview selects the current facts together and displays the final campaign, source context, origins, replacements, surviving consumer revisions, queue changes and lifecycle effects. Internal cohort prerequisites become retained binding context; external waits remain in the campaign. Active/review sources and blocking live preparation claims refuse. Active/review owners and consumers can be revised without pausing, fencing or rebinding them, leaving their accepted attempt scope stale.
5. After the read-only native preview, present a short proposal for the human's separate approval. Name the combined outcome and show one row per item being absorbed: its work name, what its checkpoint delivers, what it waits on and any exception. Explain conflicting claims, uncertain evidence and unmatched work where they affect the recommendation. Describe an individual commitment only when its proposed treatment differs from carrying it into the combined work; keep the complete per-obligation and origin map for application. State the pending choices with a recommendation for each. This human view explains the native preview's effects without copying its field inventory; nothing has been applied yet.

## Apply confirmed consolidation

Only after the human separately confirms that exact proposal, call the tool's `confirm` leaf with the same selection, returned `binding`, their exact `human_confirmation`, and your trusted current task/host identities. Confirmation rereads the selected facts under the write lock and atomically applies the whole map, including superseded source endings. Changed relevant facts require a fresh preview and separate confirmation; an unrelated project revision alone does not. Read current coverage afterward. A committed warning keeps its ledger effect: follow its view-repair instruction and do not replay confirmation. This grants no launch, rebind, completion, process or repository authority.

For an existing live replacement whose source work was carried only in prose, use ordinary `revise-item` to attach each exact retained `origin` to its carrying obligation in a complete v3 definition. Keep origin-free obligations explicit with `origin: null`; origin-free definitions use v2. Read `pinboard_obligation_coverage` after revision and reload: only direct origins for the current source revision count. Invalid origins refuse unchanged. Completed replacements remain readable coverage sources, and terminal definitions cannot be revised. Separate close and completion conditions remain unchanged.

A retrospective portfolio comparison can illustrate a recommendation, but label its historical definitions and lifecycle facts as retrospective. Dogfood the procedure separately on an explicit current live cohort and record whether the ledger revision stayed unchanged during that preview; concurrent work may advance the revision, so attribute only observed effects rather than treating equal revisions as the sole proof.

## Shape a campaign

A campaign is one consolidated item that one owner runs autonomously for a long stretch, so the human starts it once and is needed only at grouped sittings and decision batches. Put its complete proposed definition into the native preview before [separate confirmation](#apply-confirmed-consolidation):

- Review the automatically carried source obligations and exact origins. Equal statements from different sources stay separate, because each origin covers only its own source.
- Add the way-of-working obligations with `origin: null`, naming the project's scarce resource and its human steps concretely. They are an intended campaign exception to intake's rule that obligations describe only the product or repository outcome, because the way the campaign runs is what it promises the human:
  1. the first reply after starting or resuming presents the agenda: checkpoint order, offline and live work, every planned human sitting and what each decision unblocks;
  2. the brief declares the named checkpoints in order;
  3. one owner holds the scarce resource and performs only resource-bound steps, while helpers do the rest;
  4. human hands-on steps are batched into as few sittings as possible;
  5. work continues on everything not waiting; when only waiting work remains, the owner pauses with every pending step and decision in one record instead of waiting;
  6. decisions are presented as one batch per pause, each with a recommendation;
  7. reports state results against the accepted outcome, and a case the brief excluded is not reported as unchecked work unless it changes a decision the human must make.
- Record in `evidence` each absorbed item with its definition revision, so its scope, non-scope and acceptance criteria stay binding context, and copy its material exclusions. Record the human's standing instructions there in their own words, such as landing every checkpoint on one branch for one publication, the publication route, cleanup and closing absorbed items; they authorize the outer workflow and are not obligations.

In the opening agenda and later campaign reports, turn these commitments into the human's next useful picture: the accepted outcomes in checkpoint order, what can proceed now, their planned hands-on sittings, and the choices that unblock the rest. Say what each checkpoint achieves and report results against that promise. Keep the full obligation/origin inventory in application instructions; carry waits, exceptions and retained decisions into replies when they affect the human's work or choices. An excluded case belongs in a reply only when it changes a present decision.

Confirmation records replacements and ends absorbed sources as `superseded`, retaining their definitions, evidence and history. Outside consumers wait on the campaign, or on one separately approved declared checkpoint that covers all their carried prerequisite work. A checkpoint can be declared in the item definition before any brief exists; declaration does not enforce execution ordering. Whole-campaign waits remain unsatisfied until the campaign finishes. The human starts or continues the campaign through its ordinary start or attempt route.
